# 万物心理学 · Mind of Everything（EN全文）


## concepts

### Consciousness
- Our subjective awareness of self and world — the part of the mind that knows something is happening.
- Consciousness is the awareness of internal and external stimuli, ranging from awake perception to self-reflection. It is among the oldest and hardest questions in psychology: we can report what we think, yet struggle to explain how raw experience arises from neural activity (the 'hard problem').
- 标签：基础，认知
### Memory
- The ability to encode, store, and retrieve information when needed.
- Memory is often split into sensory, short-term (working) and long-term stores; long-term memory divides into declarative (facts/events) and non-declarative (skills/habits). Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve shows unreviewed material decays fast then levels off.
- 标签：认知
### Emotion
- A complex state of physiological arousal, subjective feeling and action tendency — joy, anger, fear, sadness.
- Emotion has physiological (racing heart), cognitive (appraisal of the situation) and expressive (facial) components. James–Lange theory holds bodily response precedes feeling; appraisal theory stresses that our interpretation of events determines the emotion.
- 标签：情绪，社会
### Motivation
- The internal drive that initiates, sustains and directs behavior toward a goal.
- Motivation is intrinsic (interest/meaning) or extrinsic (reward/punishment). Self-determination theory holds that autonomy, competence and relatedness fuel intrinsic motivation; expectancy theory predicts effort by value × expectation.
- 标签：动机
### Personality
- The relatively stable, cross-situational patterns of thinking, feeling and behaving.
- The Big Five (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism) is the most validated trait model. Behavior genetics suggests roughly 40–50% of personality variance is heritable, the rest from environment and experience.
- 标签：人格
### Attachment
- The enduring emotional bond between infant and primary caregiver that shapes relationships for life.
- Bowlby proposed attachment theory; Ainsworth's 'Strange Situation' identified secure, anxious-ambivalent and avoidant styles. Securely attached children explore more confidently, and adult attachment strongly affects intimate relationships.
- 标签：发展，关系
### Attribution
- How we explain the causes of behavior — disposition or situation.
- Heider proposed internal-external attribution; Kelley used consensus, distinctiveness and consistency to infer cause. The fundamental attribution error is over-crediting personality and under-crediting situation for others (calling someone 'unreliable' rather than 'stuck in traffic').
- 标签：社会
### Self
- One's sense of 'who I am' — self-concept, self-esteem and self-monitoring.
- Self-concept is the belief network about oneself; self-esteem its affective evaluation. Mead argued the self forms in social interaction; Cooley's 'looking-glass self' says we know ourselves through others' eyes. East vs. West differ in independent vs. interdependent self.
- 标签：社会，文化
### Self-Efficacy
- How strongly you believe 'I can do this' — it shapes action more than ability alone.
- Bandura: self-efficacy is your judgment about succeeding at a specific task, built from mastery, role models, persuasion and mood. High efficacy means more challenge-taking and persistence. Unlike global self-esteem, it is task-specific.
- 标签：社会，动机
### Big Five (OCEAN)
- Personality in five traits: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
- The Big Five (OCEAN) is the best-supported trait framework; dimensions are stable and measurable across cultures. It describes differences, not good/bad, and predicts health, study and work outcomes. It captures tendencies, not destiny.
- 标签：人格
### Mental Model
- The internal 'simulation' in our head of how things work — used to understand, predict and decide.
- Mental models, proposed by Craik (1943) and formalized by Johnson-Laird (1983), are small in-head models of how the world works, used to predict outcomes. They need not be accurate copies—just good enough (e.g., you 'feel' the elevator comes faster if you press again; it doesn't). Kahneman's System 1/2 and anchoring both rest on such simplified models.
- 标签：认知，决策
### MBTI (16 Personality Types)
- A popular 4-axis test sorting people into 16 types—yet scientifically contested.
- MBTI derives from Jung's psychological types, developed by Myers & Briggs; four dichotomies (E/I, S/N, T/F, J/P) yield 16 types (e.g., INTJ). It is hugely popular for being simple and non-judgmental. Yet mainstream psychology is cautious: retest reliability is modest (you may get a different type later), it splits continuous traits into either/or, and it predicts behavior/jobs worse than the Big Five. Use it as an icebreaker for self-reflection, not a scientific basis for ability or hiring.
- 标签：人格

## glossary

### Conditioning
- 定义：Learning in which a neutral stimulus comes to elicit a response through repeated pairings; split into classical and operant conditioning.
- 大白话：Pavlov's dog salivating at a bell is the classic case of a conditioned reflex.
- 标签：学习，行为
### Schema
- 定义：A mental framework (Piaget) for organizing and interpreting experience, developing through assimilation and accommodation.
- 大白话：You carry a default 'how to order at a restaurant' template in your head — that's a schema guiding new situations.
- 标签：认知
### Defense Mechanism
- 定义：Unconscious strategies (Freud & Anna Freud) that reduce anxiety and inner conflict — repression, projection, sublimation.
- 大白话：Reframing an unbearable desire as 'I don't care anyway' is a defense that shields you from pain.
- 标签：精神分析
### Cognitive Dissonance
- 定义：The discomfort when attitudes conflict with behavior (or with each other), driving change to restore consistency.
- 大白话：Knowing smoking harms yet still smoking feels off — to ease it you may quietly tell yourself 'the risk is exaggerated'.
- 标签：社会，动机
### Priming
- 定义：Prior exposure to a stimulus unconsciously influences later processing of related stimuli, often outside awareness.
- 大白话：After seeing 'doctor', you're more likely to complete '__or' as 'doctor' — a hidden association lit up.
- 标签：认知
### Reinforcement
- 定义：In operant conditioning, adding a pleasant stimulus (positive) or removing an aversive one (negative) to increase a behavior's future odds.
- 大白话：A sticker for tidying (positive) or skipping dishes after homework (negative) both 'add incentive' for good behavior.
- 标签：行为
### Learned Helplessness
- 定义：After repeated uncontrollable bad events, the passive state of giving up even when change becomes possible.
- 大白话：A caged dog shocked with no escape later won't flee even with the door open — it 'learned' helplessness. Humans under chronic failure do too.
- 标签：行为，情绪
### Theory of Mind
- 定义：The ability to attribute different beliefs, intentions and knowledge to others and predict behavior from them; matures around age 4.
- 大白话：Realizing 'mom can't see the candy, so she doesn't know where it is' — that insight that others' minds differ is theory of mind.
- 标签：发展，社会

## psychologists

### Sigmund Freud
- 年代：1856–1939
- 领域：Psychoanalysis
- Proposed the unconscious and the id/ego/superego structure, founded the talking cure, and reshaped how we see the irrational self.
- Freud held that much mental life is unconscious and childhood conflict surfaces as neurosis. He developed free association and dream analysis around sex and aggression drives. Weak falsifiability and narrow samples make him contested, yet 'the unconscious' now pervades culture and clinic.
- 要点：Unconscious；Id / Ego / Superego；Defense mechanisms；Psychosexual stages
- 著作：The Interpretation of Dreams；The Ego and the Id
- 标签：精神分析，临床
### Jean Piaget
- 年代：1896–1980
- 领域：Developmental Psychology
- Explained how children actively build understanding via schema-assimilation-accommodation, proposing four stages of cognitive development.
- Piaget argued children are not small adults but understand the world via distinct structures: sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete and formal operations. His clinical interviews revealed reasoning errors and founded genetic epistemology, shaping education and toy design.
- 要点：Schema；Assimilation & Accommodation；Stages of development；Genetic epistemology
- 著作：The Principles of Genetic Epistemology
- 标签：发展，认知
### B. F. Skinner
- 年代：1904–1990
- 领域：Behaviorism
- Explained behavior via operant conditioning and reinforcement schedules, arguing 'environment shapes behavior', influencing programmed instruction and behavior modification.
- Skinner separated respondent (classical) from operant (consequence-controlled) behavior and studied schedules (fixed/variable ratio) shaping response rates. He rejected inner mental entities, holding that prediction and control need only observable stimulus-response-reinforcement chains.
- 要点：Operant conditioning；Reinforcement schedules；Shaping；Radical behaviorism
- 著作：Science and Human Behavior；Beyond Freedom and Dignity
- 标签：行为，学习
### Albert Bandura
- 年代：1925–2021
- 领域：Social / Cognitive
- Proposed observational learning and self-efficacy, showing people are shaped not only by reinforcement but by watching others and believing in their own capability.
- Bandura's Bobo doll experiment showed children imitate adult aggression; he held behavior, cognition and environment co-determine each other (triadic reciprocity). Self-efficacy — belief in one's capability — is a core antecedent of motivation and persistence.
- 要点：Observational learning；Self-efficacy；Reciprocal determinism；Modeling
- 著作：Self-Efficacy: The Exercise of Control
- 标签：社会，学习
### Daniel Kahneman
- 年代：1934–2024
- 领域：Cognitive Psychology / Behavioral Economics
- Explained how humans switch between intuition and reason via System 1 / System 2, revealing a family of stable judgment biases.
- With Tversky, Kahneman proposed prospect theory showing systematic departures from rationality under uncertainty (loss aversion, framing). 'Thinking, Fast and Slow' brought heuristics and biases from lab to public and policy.
- 要点：System 1 / System 2；Prospect theory；Heuristics & biases；Loss aversion
- 著作：Thinking, Fast and Slow
- 标签：认知，决策
### Abraham Maslow
- 年代：1908–1970
- 领域：Humanistic Psychology
- Used the 'hierarchy of needs' pyramid to show people seek safety first, then belonging, esteem and self-actualization.
- A founder of humanistic psychology, Maslow rejected-only-pathology or mechanical views, stressing potential, agency and growth. The hierarchy is famous yet weakly evidenced, and 'self-actualization' is loosely defined.
- 要点：Hierarchy of needs；Self-actualization；Humanism；Peak experience
- 著作：Motivation and Personality
- 标签：人本，动机
### Ivan Pavlov
- 年代：1849–1936
- 领域：Physiology / Behavior
- With dogs' salivation, discovered classical conditioning — that association can reshape physiological response.
- A physiologist studying digestion, Pavlov found by chance that after pairing a bell with food, dogs salivated to the bell alone. This 'conditioned response' became a cornerstone of behaviorism and later fear-conditioning research.
- 要点：Classical conditioning；Unconditioned / conditioned stimulus；Associative learning
- 著作：Conditioned Reflexes
- 标签：行为，生理
### Paul Ekman
- 年代：1934–
- 领域：Emotion / Nonverbal
- Argued basic emotions and their facial expressions are universal across cultures, and built tools to read micro-expressions.
- Across Papua New Guinea and Western samples, Ekman found six expressions (joy, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise) recognized consistently, supporting universality. His FACS coding system is widely used in psychology and deception research.
- 要点：Universality of basic emotions；Facial expression；Micro-expression；FACS coding
- 著作：Emotions Revealed；Telling Lies
- 标签：情绪，社会

## schools

### Psychoanalysis
- 年代：19世纪末–20世纪中叶
- Centers on the unconscious, childhood conflict and drives, using the talking cure to probe deep motives.
- 要点：Unconscious；Id/Ego/Superego；Defense mechanisms；Dream analysis
- 代表：Sigmund Freud；Anna Freud；Carl Jung
- 标签：精神分析，临床
### Behaviorism
- 年代：1913–1970s
- Studies only observable stimulus-response, explaining all behavior by learning laws, rejecting inner mental entities.
- 要点：Classical conditioning；Operant conditioning；Reinforcement；Shaping
- 代表：John Watson；B. F. Skinner；Ivan Pavlov
- 标签：行为，学习
### Cognitive Psychology
- 年代：1950s–至今
- Treats the mind as an information-processing system, studying attention, memory, language and problem solving.
- 要点：Information processing；Schema；Working memory；Priming
- 代表：Jean Piaget；Ulric Neisser；George Miller
- 标签：认知
### Humanistic Psychology
- 年代：1950s–至今
- Stresses human agency, potential and dignity, focusing on growth, self-actualization and the genuine therapeutic bond.
- 要点：Self-actualization；Hierarchy of needs；Unconditional positive regard；Client-centered
- 代表：Abraham Maslow；Carl Rogers；Rollo May
- 标签：人本，临床
### Evolutionary Psychology
- 年代：1980s–至今
- Explains the origin of mental mechanisms via natural selection, seeing many tendencies as products of ancestral adaptive problems.
- 要点：Adaptation；Modular mind；Mating strategies；Reciprocal altruism
- 代表：David Buss；Leda Cosmides；John Tooby
- 标签：进化，社会
### Neuropsychology
- 年代：1980s–至今
- Locates mental processes in specific brain regions and circuits via neuroscience and neuroimaging.
- 要点：Localization；Synaptic plasticity；Emotion & reason；Split-brain
- 代表：Antonio Damasio；Michael Gazzaniga；Donald Hebb
- 标签：神经，认知
### Positive Psychology
- 年代：1998–至今
- Not only pathology, but systematically studies happiness, strengths, resilience and meaning — 'what makes life worth living'.
- 要点：Learned optimism；Flow；Character strengths；PERMA model
- 代表：Martin Seligman；Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi；Christopher Peterson
- 标签：积极，幸福
### Structuralism
- 年代：1879–1920s
- Used introspection to break consciousness into basic elements, aiming for a 'periodic table' of the mind.
- 要点：Introspection；Elements of consciousness；Sensations & images；Experimental psychology
- 代表：Wilhelm Wundt；Edward Titchener
- 标签：基础，早期

## biases

### Confirmation Bias
- We favor evidence that supports what we already believe and dismiss the rest.
- How it shows: reading only like-minded media, recalling hits but forgetting misses. Why: the brain saves energy and protects identity. How to avoid: seek disconfirming evidence; ask 'where might I be wrong?'
### Anchoring
- The first number we see drags later judgments, even if irrelevant.
- How it shows: strike-through prices, opening high in negotiation. Why: System 1 sticks to the anchor and under-adjusts. How to avoid: set your own fair range first, compare across sources.
### Availability Heuristic
- What comes easily to mind feels more common.
- How it shows: fearing flying after a headline crash while driving daily. Why: vividness stands in for base rates. How to avoid: rely on statistics over impressions.
### Loss Aversion
- The pain of losing 100 feels about twice the joy of gaining 100.
- How it shows: holding losing stocks, refusing a better deal to avoid loss. Why: avoiding loss aided survival. How to avoid: weigh the cost of inaction too.
### Bandwagon Effect
- The more people believe something, the likelier we follow—even if all are wrong.
- How it shows: panic buying, herd investing, mobbing. Why: need to belong and the shortcut 'majority = right'. How to avoid: keep space to think alone first.
### Sunk Cost Fallacy
- Money or time already spent and unrecoverable shouldn't shape future choices.
- How it shows: finishing a bad film, propping a failing project. Why: reluctance to admit waste. How to avoid: ask only 'is continuing worth it now?'
### Halo Effect
- If someone excels at one thing, we assume they're good at everything.
- How it shows: attractive people judged smarter; elite alumni trusted more. Why: global impression substitutes for itemized judgment. How to avoid: evaluate traits separately.
### Dunning-Kruger Effect
- The least skilled overestimate most; the most skilled underestimate.
- How it shows: novices overconfident, experts cautious. Why: the unskilled also lack the skill to see it. How to avoid: treat 'I don't know' as a start; learn from better performers.

## experiments

### Stanford Prison Experiment
- Ordinary students assigned as guards or prisoners 'became' their roles within days.
- Hypothesis: situation beats personality. Procedure: Zimbardo turned a basement into a prison; 24 drew roles; stopped after 6 days as abuse escalated. Conclusion: context strongly shapes behavior, but the sample was tiny and guided—contested. Ethics: deception and harm; such studies are now tightly restricted.
### Milgram Obedience Experiment
- Under 'authority', participants delivered (fake) lethal shocks to a stranger.
- Hypothesis: ordinary people obey harmful orders. Procedure: a 'learner' was shocked up to 450V on command. Conclusion: about two-thirds went to the max. Ethics: heavy deception and stress, inspiring modern ethics rules.
### Marshmallow Test
- Kids who delayed a marshmallow seemed to do better later—maybe.
- Hypothesis: self-control predicts later success. Procedure: a child could wait 15 min for two treats. Conclusion: delay correlated with later outcomes. Contested: later work shows background matters more; the correlation was overstated.
### Little Albert
- A baby was conditioned to fear furry things with a loud noise—fear can be learned.
- Hypothesis: emotions are learnable by conditioning. Procedure: a loud clang paired with a rat; Albert soon cried at fur. Conclusion: supports behaviorism. Ethics: induced lasting fear with no desensitization—unacceptable today.
### Asch Conformity
- People picked the wrong line just to match the group.
- Hypothesis: group pressure bends obvious judgments. Procedure: confederates gave wrong line comparisons. Conclusion: ~1/3 conformed at least once; one dissenter sharply cut conformity. Lesson: dissent is costly but vital.
### Pavlov's Dog
- A bell alone made dogs salivate—neutral cues can trigger responses.
- Hypothesis: responses are learned by pairing stimuli. Procedure: a bell before food; soon the bell alone salivated the dog. Conclusion: classical conditioning holds, seeding behaviorism. Extension: ads and first-impression liking use the same link.
### Cognitive Dissonance
- Those paid little for a boring task found it more interesting.
- Hypothesis: conflicting attitude and behavior creates discomfort, so we change the attitude. Procedure: after a dull task, some were paid $1, some $20, to lie it was fun. Conclusion: the $1 group convinced themselves it was fun (insufficient justification). Lesson: small self-chosen costs shift beliefs most.

## timeline

### Wundt founds the first psychology lab
- Psychology split from philosophy into an experimental science of the mind.
- At Leipzig, Wundt used introspection and reaction-time measures—the birth of scientific psychology.
### Freud's 'The Interpretation of Dreams'
- Put the unconscious on stage; founded psychoanalysis.
- Argued the unconscious, childhood and drives shape behavior; influential yet hard to test empirically.
### Watson launches Behaviorism
- 'Study only observable behavior'—banned introspection from science.
- Behaviorism stressed stimulus-response and learning, seeding conditioning research and later the cognitive turn.
### Piaget's child cognitive development
- Children don't think like small adults; they build thought in stages.
- Four stages—sensorimotor to formal operations—founded developmental psychology.
### Skinner's operant conditioning
- Rewards and punishments shape behavior; schedules set the pace.
- The Skinner box systematically studied reinforcement schedules, influencing teaching, management and therapy.
### Maslow's hierarchy of needs
- From safety to self-actualization, needs climb like a ladder.
- Humanistic psychology rose, stressing growth and agency, not just pathology.
### The Cognitive Revolution
- Mind re-read as an information processor; the black box opened.
- Chomsky, Simon and others brought memory, attention and language back; behaviorism receded.
### Bandura's social learning (Bobo doll)
- Kids who saw adults hit a doll copied it—learning by watching.
- Introduced vicarious reinforcement and self-efficacy, bridging behaviorism and cognition.
### Kahneman & Tversky: Prospect Theory
- People aren't rational calculators; decisions are systematically biased.
- Foundation of behavioral economics; brought psychology into decision research; Nobel in 2002 (Kahneman).
### The Brain-imaging Era
- fMRI let 'mental activity' be seen for the first time.
- Cognitive neuroscience rose; emotion, memory and choice gained spatial maps, plus new debates like neuromarketing.
### Rise of Positive Psychology
- Not just treating illness, but studying 'how to live well'.
- Seligman pushed well-being, strengths and flow, balancing the pathology focus.
### AI Meets Psychology
- LLMs are both a research subject and a tool—with fresh ethics questions.
- AI for cognitive modeling, emotional support, screening aids; plus risks of privacy, dependency and misuse. Not a diagnosis here.

## everyday

### Procrastination
- Not laziness—present feelings override the future self.
- Why: facing a dreaded task, the brain flees for quick relief, reinforcing the habit. What helps: shrink tasks to 'start in 5 min'; reward after, not before; accept an imperfect first draft.
### Decision Fatigue
- Too many choices in a day makes later ones careless or impulsive.
- How it shows: can't pick dinner, impulse buys, short temper. Why: self-control is a limited, depletable resource. What helps: decide big things in the morning; automate small choices; schedule decision-free time.
### Social Media & Mood
- More scrolling, more hollow: comparison and algorithms drain you.
- Mechanism: others' highlights trigger upward comparison; dopamine loops keep you in. What helps: kill notifications, set time windows; create instead of scroll; no screens an hour before bed.
### Relationships & Attachment
- How we react in love often replays childhood attachment.
- Three patterns: secure (depend and be alone), anxious (fear abandonment, need reassurance), avoidant (distant, fear engulfment). What helps: name your pattern; say needs plainly instead of testing; don't rush to label the other.
### Burnout
- Not fixed by sleep—chronic depletion from loss of control.
- Three signs: exhaustion, detachment, low efficacy. Why: demand outruns resources, little autonomy. What helps: reclaim small control (set your own priorities); set boundaries; discuss load if needed. Not a diagnosis here.
### Praise in Parenting
- Praising effort beats praising smarts for resilience.
- Mechanism: praising talent breeds fear of failure; praising effort treats hard tasks as practice (growth mindset). What helps: name the behavior ('you stayed focused'), avoid 'smart/dumb' labels.
### Sleep & Memory
- In sleep, the brain files away the day's information.
- Mechanism: deep sleep consolidates memory and clears waste; lack of it weakens learning and frays mood. What helps: regular sleep, less screen light; don't pull all-nighters—sleep is review.

## myths

### Left-brain / Right-brain People
- No one uses just one hemisphere;分工 is rough and whole-brain by default.
- Truth: hemispheres do differ roughly (e.g. language leans left), but any complex task uses both. 'Left=logic, right=creative' is a marketing simplification. Source: imaging shows whole-brain activation.
### Blood Type Personality
- Blood type and personality are unrelated—pop culture, not science.
- Truth: large studies find no stable link between blood type and traits; the belief is popular in Japan/Korea dating and hiring. Don't label people by it.
### Multiple Intelligences Test
- Gardner's 'multiple intelligences' is a framework, not a precise test.
- Truth: the theory broadened 'intelligence beyond IQ', but commercial 'tests' often lack validity and shouldn't sort careers or classes. Use as a lens, not a verdict.
### We Only Use 10% of Our Brain
- The whole brain burns oxygen nearly all the time; 10% is a myth.
- Truth: imaging shows widespread activity even at rest; damage to any region causes deficits. Source: neuroscience consensus.
### Memory Is Like a Recording
- Each recall rewrites memory, mixing in later information.
- Truth: memory is reconstructive; wording and others' suggestions reshape it (false memories). Source: Loftus on the misinformation effect.
### Boys and Girls Learn Differently
- Sex differences in brain are far smaller than person-to-person ones; segregated teaching lacks basis.
- Truth: 'male/female brain' mostly reflects tiny group averages, useless per person; sex-segregated teaching isn't proven better. Source: meta-analyses on sex and cognition.

## quiz

### Only reading like-minded news is called what?
- A common habit that narrows your view.
- Confirmation bias: we favor evidence supporting existing beliefs and ignore the rest. Counter it by seeking disconfirming evidence.
### Which experiment had ~2/3 obey 'authority' to 'lethal' shocks?
- A classic social study of obedience.
- Milgram's obedience experiment (1963): ordinary people went to the max voltage on command (fake). It spurred modern ethics rules.
### The claim 'we only use 10% of our brain' is...
- A widely spread claim.
- A myth. Imaging shows widespread activity even at rest; damage to any region causes deficits.
### The pain of losing 100 feels about how many times the joy of gaining 100?
- A core finding about risk preference.
- About twice—loss aversion, from Kahneman & Tversky's prospect theory.
### Which praise better builds resilience in kids?
- A repeatedly tested difference in parenting.
- Praising effort beats praising smarts: a growth mindset treats hard tasks as practice, not a threat to talent.
### East Asian cultures lean toward which 'self'?
- A classic cross-cultural distinction.
- Interdependent self: the 'I' sits in roles and relationships, valuing harmony and duty—the collectivist end of the spectrum.
### The acronym 'OCEAN' refers to which personality framework?
- The mainstream model that sums up personality in five letters.
- The Big Five: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism—the best-validated trait framework, describing differences not good/bad.
### Behavior genetics suggests about what share of personality variance is heritable?
- A rough nature-vs-nurture share.
- Roughly 40%–50%. The rest comes from environment, experience and chance; personality is relatively, not absolutely, stable and can shift somewhat in adulthood.
### After a lively party you feel drained and want to be alone — this more likely means?
- A common difference in where energy comes from.
- You may lean introverted: introverts recharge in solitude, extraverts in social buzz. It is a tendency, not a skill gap or disorder. The Big Five describes tendencies, not labels.
### You prefer 'plan ahead' vs 'go with the flow' — this mainly reflects which difference?
- Which dimension planning belongs to.
- Conscientiousness in the Big Five: planning, order, reliability. It has nothing to do with blood type or left/right brain (both are myths). High conscientiousness predicts on-time, fewer slips—but only probabilistically.
### On 'personality tests, astrology, blood type', psychology leans toward which view?
- The line between science and pseudoscience.
- Trait scales like the Big Five describe stable average tendencies and are cross-culturally validated, but they don't define a person or constitute diagnosis. Astrology and blood-type-personality lack empirical support—pop culture, not science. Personality is relatively, not absolutely, fixed.
### On MBTI (16 types) vs the Big Five, what does mainstream psychology lean toward?
- The gap between a popular tool and a scientific framework.
- The Big Five is favored for stability, cross-cultural validity and predictive power; MBTI is easy, non-judgmental and good for icebreaking, but less reliable and splits continuous traits into either/or. Both aid self-reflection, yet MBTI shouldn't be a scientific basis for ability or hiring.

## compare

### Self-Construal: Independent vs Interdependent
- The West leans independent self; East Asia leans interdependent self.
- West (independent): uniqueness, self-expression, personal goals first. China/East Asia (interdependent): roles, harmony, relational duties. Impact: self-evaluation, communication, even memory (East Asians recall 'with whom' contexts).
### Individualism vs Collectivism
- One end prizes individual rights, the other group belonging—both lie on a spectrum.
- The West leans individualistic: competition, privacy, directness. East Asia leans collectivist: consensus, face, the bigger picture. Note: urban youth today mix more—don't replace a person with a label.
### Emotion Expression: Expressive vs Restrained
- The West encourages saying feelings; East Asia weighs occasion and 'face'.
- West: expressing feelings reads as sincerity and healing. China: expression weighs relationship and occasion; restraint reads as maturity; open conflict is avoided. Different rules, not different capacity to feel.
### Parenting: Autonomy vs Training
- The West leans 'let them choose'; East Asia leans 'pave the long road, value discipline'.
- West: child autonomy, interest-led, less intervention. China: academic investment, long planning, strict parenting, also closer attachment. Research: security matters more than 'which rules'.
### Conformity: Uniqueness vs Belonging
- The West prizes 'not following'; East Asia restrains to 'not offend'.
- Western culture rewards 'standing out'; East Asia wary of the relational cost of 'sticking out'. Both conform—just the allowed range differs.
### Seeking Help: Open vs Reserved
- The West treats help-seeking as self-care; East Asia fears 'airing dirty laundry'.
- West: counseling feels like fitness. China: stigma and 'tough it out' delay care. In any culture, earlier help helps more—this site informs, not replaces professional assessment.
